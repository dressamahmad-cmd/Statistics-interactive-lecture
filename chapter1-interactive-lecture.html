1.2

Types of Statistical Applications in Business

Section 1.2 · الإحصاء الوصفي والإحصاء الاستدلالي

Statistics involves two different processes:

① Describing sets of data

→ Descriptive statistics

② Drawing conclusions

(making estimates, decisions, predictions, etc.) → Inferential statistics

So, the applications of statistics can be divided into two broad areas: descriptive statistics and inferential statistics.

Branches of statistics

Descriptive statistics

Descriptive statistics utilizes numerical and graphical methods to explore data, i.e., to look for patterns in a data set, to summarize the information revealed in a data set, and to present the information in a convenient form.

For example:

  • The average age of the students is 14 years.
  • The median household income for people aged 25–34 is $35,888.

Inferential statistics

Inferential statistics utilizes sample data to make estimates, decisions, predictions, or other generalizations about a larger set of data.

For example:

  • The relationship between smoking and lung cancer.
  • Probability.

Fundamental Elements of Statistics

Section 1.3 · العناصر الأساسية: الوحدة والمجتمع والمتغير والعينة

Six words every study uses

TermDefinition (from the lecture)
Experimental unit
(الوحدة التجريبية)		Also called observational unit: an object (e.g., person, thing, transaction, or event) upon which we collect data.
Population
(المجتمع)		A set (or collection) of all units (usually people, objects, transactions, or events) that we are interested in studying.
Variable
(المتغير)		A characteristic or property of an individual experimental unit.
Measurement
(القياس)		The process we use to assign numbers to variables of individual population units.
Census
(الحصر الشامل)		Measuring a variable for every experimental unit of a population.
Sample
(العينة)		A subset (or portion/part) of the units of a population.

Studying all units of a population would be prohibitively time consuming and/or costly. A reasonable alternative is to select and study a subset of all units in the population.

1.5

Types of Data

Section 1.5 · أنواع البيانات: وصفية وكمية

Data, data set, data value

Data are obtained by measuring the values of one or more variables on the units in the sample (or population).
Data set is the collection of data values.  ·  Data value is each value in the data set.

🗂️ Click a term to see it on the table

💡 شرح مبسط
الجدول: كل عمود = متغير (Age, Gender, Nationality). كل خلية = قيمة بيانات (Data value). الجدول كله = مجموعة البيانات (Data set). و"Data" = القيم المقاسة لمتغير ما (مثل عمود الجنسية).

Types of data

Quantitative data

Measurements recorded on a naturally occurring numerical scale.
  1. The temperature (in degrees Celsius) at which each unit in a sample of 20 pieces of heat-resistant plastic begins to melt.
  2. The current unemployment rate (measured as a percentage) for each of the 50 states.
  3. The scores of a sample of 150 MBA applicants on the GMAT, a standardized business school entrance exam.
  4. The number of female executives employed in each of a sample of 75 manufacturing companies.

Qualitative data

Measurements that cannot be measured on a natural numerical scale; they can only be classified into one of a group of categories. Also named categorical data.
  1. The political party affiliation (Democrat, Republican, or Independent) in a sample of 50 CEOs.
  2. The defective status (defective or not) of each of 100 computer chips manufactured by Intel.
  3. The size of a car (subcompact, compact, midsize, or full-size) rented by each of a sample of 30 business travelers.
  4. A taste tester’s ranking (best, worst, etc.) of four brands of barbecue sauce for a panel of 10 testers.
💡 شرح مبسط
اختبار سريع: هل يمكن أن أضع القيمة على مقياس رقمي طبيعي وأحسب لها متوسطًا؟ نعم ← كمية. هل هي فئة أو صفة؟ ← وصفية.
انتبه: الأرقام قد تكون رموزًا لفئات (رقم الهاتف، رقم الطالب) فتبقى وصفية.
➕ إضافة (ليست في الشرائح الأصلية): تقسيم البيانات الكمية إلى منفصلة (Discrete) وهي ما نعدّه مثل عدد المتاجر، ومتصلة (Continuous) وهي ما نقيسه مثل الوزن والزمن، إضافة مني، والشرائح تذكر الكلمتين فقط ضمن خيارات سؤال.

Why “true zero” matters

1.6

Collecting Data

Section 1.6 · طرق جمع البيانات

Once you decide on the type of data (quantitative or qualitative) appropriate for the problem at hand, you will need to collect the data. Generally, there are three different ways to obtain it:

1. Published source

Data collected for you already.

2. Designed experiment

The researcher controls the units.

3. Observational study

Units are observed in their natural setting (e.g., a survey).

1. Published source

Sometimes the data set of interest has already been collected for you and is available in a published source, such as a book, journal, newspaper, or Web site.

For example: you may want to examine and summarize the unemployment rates (percentages of eligible workers who are unemployed) in the 50 states of the United States. You can find this data set (as well as numerous other data sets) at the library in the Statistical Abstract of the United States, published annually by the U.S. government.

2. Designed experiment

A designed experiment is a data-collection method where the researcher exerts full control over the characteristics of the experimental units (people, objects, or events) in the study.

3. Observational study

An observational study is a data-collection method where the experimental units sampled are observed in their natural setting. No attempt is made to control the characteristics of the experimental units sampled. (Examples include opinion polls and surveys.)
Survey: the most common type of observational study, where the researcher samples a group of people, asks one or more questions, and records the responses.

Sampling Techniques

Section 1.6 · أنواع العينات العشوائية

Four ways to take a random-type sample: Random Systematic Stratified Cluster

1. Random sampling

A random sample is a sample in which all members of the population have an equal chance of being selected. Members are selected by using chance methods or random numbers.

2. Systematic sampling

A systematic sample is a sample obtained by selecting every k-th member of the population, where k is a counting number.
k = N / n, where n is the sample size and N is the population size.

3. Stratified samples

A stratified sample is obtained by dividing the population into subgroups (strata) according to some characteristic relevant to the study. Then subjects are selected from each subgroup.

For example: a researcher selects a random sample from each gender to check their blood pressure.

4. Cluster samples

A cluster sample is obtained by dividing the population into sections or clusters, then selecting one or more clusters and using all members in the cluster(s) as the members of the sample.

For example: in a large school district, all teachers from two buildings are interviewed to determine whether they believe the students have less homework to do now than in previous years.

• عنقودية Cluster: نختار مجموعات كاملة عشوائيًا ونأخذ كل أفرادها (بعض المجموعات لا تظهر أبدًا).

Summary of sampling techniques

TechniqueHow subjects are selected
Random samplingSubjects are selected by a random number generator.
Systematic samplingSelect every k-th subject after the 1st subject is selected from 1 to k.
Stratified samplingDivide the population into subgroups called “strata”, then subjects are selected randomly within each group.
Cluster samplingSubjects are selected by using intact subgroups randomly.
🧠 للحفظ
سؤال واحد يحسم أغلب الحالات: هل استخدمتُ كل أفراد مجموعة كاملة؟ نعم ← عنقودية. هل أخذت عينة من كل مجموعة؟ ← طبقية. كل k-th؟ ← منتظمة. أرقام عشوائية؟ ← عشوائية.

1.6

Problems with Nonrandom Samples

Section 1.6 · مشكلات العينات غير العشوائية

When a sample is not random, three problems may spoil the results: Selection bias Nonresponse bias Measurement error

1. Selection bias

Selection bias results when a subset of experimental units in the population has little or no chance of being selected for the sample.

2. Nonresponse bias

Nonresponse bias is a type of selection bias that results when the response data differ from the potential data for the nonresponders.

3. Measurement error

Measurement error refers to inaccuracies in the values of the data collected. In surveys, the error may be due to ambiguous or leading questions and the interviewer’s effect on the respondent.
💡 شرح مبسط
تشبيه: تريد معرفة طعم قدر الشوربة.
• انحياز الاختيار: تتذوق من الطبقة العلوية فقط (جزء من القدر لا فرصة له). • انحياز عدم الاستجابة: اخترت الجميع لكن من أجابوا يختلفون عمّن لم يجيبوا. • خطأ القياس: لسانك أو سؤالك نفسه يُخطئ (سؤال إيحائي أو غامض).

1.7

Business Analytics: Critical Thinking with Statistics

Section 1.7 · تحليلات الأعمال والتفكير الإحصائي

Business analytics refers to methodologies (e.g., statistical methods) that extract useful information from data in order to make better business decisions.
Statistical thinking involves applying rational thought and the science of statistics to critically assess data and inferences. Fundamental to the thought process is that variation exists in populations and process data.

Summary and Final Challenge

Putting it all together · الخلاصة والتحدي الأخير

📌 Summary

Statistics

The science of data: collecting, classifying, summarizing, organizing, analyzing, interpreting.

Two branches

Descriptive: summarizes data. Inferential: uses a sample to generalize, estimate, predict.

Elements

Unit, population, variable, measurement, census, sample.

Data types

Qualitative (categories) and quantitative (numbers).

Scales

Nominal → ordinal → interval → ratio.

Collecting

Published source, designed experiment, observational study (survey).

Sampling

Random, systematic (k = N/n), stratified, cluster.

Problems

Selection bias, nonresponse bias, measurement error.

Analytics

Statistics turns data into better decisions; variation exists.

Easily confused pairs

PairThe difference
Population vs sampleAll units vs a part of them.
Census vs sampleMeasure every unit vs measure a subset.
Stratified vs clusterSample from every group vs take whole groups.
Interval vs ratioNo true zero vs true zero.
Nominal vs ordinalNo order vs ordered categories.
Selection vs nonresponse biasSome units cannot be chosen vs chosen units do not answer.
